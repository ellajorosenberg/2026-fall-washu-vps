My website is a shifting house next to a river of knowledge. What could yours be?

This article really put things in perspective for me and made me think about websites in a different way than I have before. The basic definition of a website, “a file or bundle of files living on a server somewhere” has so much more to consider, but is so vague that it encompasses anything. The quote “links are the oxygen of the web” was the beginning of the metaphorical language. In this case, links are what connect everything and are the basis of a website and interactive elements.

I liked what Laurel said about artists breaking out of the box and not limiting themself to just 1 website, and also taking advantage of the ability to create anything. Websites are “both subject and object at once,” which allows artists (or anyone!) to create anything to display anything—it’s so open, and the possibilities are endless. The website creator “becomes both author and architect simultaneously,” and can craft their website to be anything they want—they have creative liberty in a world where there are no limits (relatively).

All of the metaphors for websites really stuck out to me and challenged the way that I currently view websites and the internet. A website is a house where many things live inside made me think about the job of a house—to provide shelter, house all of your belongings, host people, etc. The house metaphor feels very fitting to me. Other metaphors, including a river, a plant that can’t be rushed and takes time to grow, and a puddle as a temporary collection of water, were an interesting way to tie something digital to physical, concrete things.

The note about the future of the web and websites being up to individuals rather than big corporations stood out to me. As the world has become more modern, we have started seeing a shift of things looking more and more alike and losing their individuality. For websites, when the big corporations take over, things start looking the same. That’s why individuals need to create unique websites that show personality and character, rather than standardized templates that are visually indistinguishable.
