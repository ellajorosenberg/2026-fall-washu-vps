The New Turing Test

I really enjoyed reading this article, especially during a time when it is so relevant. I resonated with the feeling of scrolling on social media and wondering whether it’s AI or not—then quickly running to the comments to validate my thoughts. It’s interesting to think about AI this way and ask not just whether it’s good or bad overall, but what specifically poses challenges and ethical concerns. In cases like on social media, it isn’t necessarily about the content, but it’s about deception. One quote that stood out to me was that a chatbot “is in fact the polar opposite of self: a regression to the mean of human expression,” which represents the moral opposition to using a non-human entity to represent the self—when it can’t possibly do that.

The rubric focuses on characteristics that make something feel human: having a unique perspective of the creator driven by complex emotions, designed for a particular audience using context, and a focus on expression and authenticity. When a chatbot creates something, it negates the personality of the individual lens that the work should come from. My main takeaways from this article are that it’s all about finding the emotional resonance rather than trying to fit a certain aesthetic or trend. It’s also an interesting idea to reframe how we address AI as the enemy and rather shift the focus to its exploitation and personal loss.
