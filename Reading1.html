Why Restaurant Websites...

This is a reading response about restaurant websites from 2019.

I really enjoyed this authentic conversation, which delves into different people’s perspectives on restaurant websites. I appreciated that each person had a different take, and that this really came from a place of real experience—much of which felt relatable. As I read this, I found myself agreeing with certain points like checking menus before going to a restaurant, and disliking when restaurants exclusively use Facebook for their menu. In my personal experience, I think that a restaurant’s website can give good insight as to the quality and aesthetic of an establishment—although the design choices can be misleading or misrepresentative of a restaurant, either wrongly convincing people to try it or inadvertently turning people away. For example, if a restaurant’s website looks dingy or “sketchy,” I might not be as interested in going than if it were aesthetic or had lots of character. Toph’s point about keeping the websites updated made me think about how that reflects consistency and commitment to diners, and is something I never really considered before. Of the websites shown, I really enjoyed DIMES and Kajitsu’s creative take on their websites, which used space, layout, and color to leverage a creative and organized feel. I noticed that some of the websites (DIMES and Kajitsu) are actually different now (since 2019), which was an interesting nod to how websites change over time.
