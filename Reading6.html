Calm Technology

I enjoyed this reading because it really painted a picture of how technology has changed our society, and how what was once a phenomenon is now required to live a “normal” life. Calm technology is the idea that technology should operate in the background of our lives, rather than being the main focus. We should use it to inform what we do, not to overwhelm us or completely take over. The purpose of technology is to improve our relationship with the world. Good technology does not necessarily demand our attention, but it supports us. What stood out to me was the way that the article put the history of technology into perspective. When computers were first introduced, they were very expensive and huge; the ratio was one computer to many people. Then, the personal computer became popular, which was one person to one computer. Nowadays, it’s many computers to one person, or many people to many computers. We have gotten so used to technology that most people now own many devices: laptop, desktop, phone, ipad, watch, headphones, etc. We rely on a whole technological ecosystem in a very different way from the past. Technology is embedded in everyday objects and environments—walls, furniture, clothing, appliances, etc. Although it can be helpful, it begs the question: is this necessary? For example, of course, having an app to control your washing machine from afar would be nice! But is it really necessary? What ever happened to doing things on our own? We interact with many, many devices without consciously recognizing them as computers, which proves that they have become so ingrained in our lives, enough that we don’t even think about it anymore. Devices require our attention—notifications, apps and services, and needing to monitor our online presence. This relates to the idea of central vs. periphery. The article explains how periphery is the information that we are aware of without actively thinking about it. We can process more info without becoming overwhelmed in our periphery. That way, we can take control of the information when we need to, but it can live in the background when we don’t. I love the example of driving. We have to focus on keeping the car in the center of the lane, but at the same time, we need to be aware of our peripheral surroundings to be safe. This analogy does a great job of explaining the importance of keeping information separate in central and peripheral, to be accessible but not always in the forefront. It shows that they are each important in different ways.
