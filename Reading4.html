The Forbidden Zone

I found this reading really interesting and found it to be a refreshing perspective. I myself am someone who likes to organize things, and I can definitely agree with the idea that it's easier to be organized when there's an established system or containers (though not for everyone). I also find fun and interesting ways to create my own systems and containers to keep things in order, whether it's organization for my room or closet, or for artwork or collections of items.

While I think Damon has an interesting view---he made his website first, then pondered what to share on it---I think I typically think the opposite way, strating with the content, then building the website. I think it's a really cool way to think about it, and it's especially relevant to this new project, because it begs the question of whether the structure should come first or the content should come first (ah, the old chicken or the egg debate).

I liked Damon's explanation of how he decides which work to put on which platform, because it shows a little bit into his thought process of how people perceive things and why certain works may belong "better" in one place than another. To me, this also relates to the idea of possibility and free will. Most things in our lives come with options, and it's a matter of making choice after choice that determines how we live and what our life looks like. In this case, perhaps uploading a specific work to a specific channel could have a different impact than if it were put somewhere else. Damon echoes this point when he says "In a strange sense, I don’t think they matter that much. I would have encountered something else, and it would have done a similar thing, perhaps." I also really enjoy his quote, "I think there are “thing-shaped holes” in each person. Other things might have just as equally as filled those holes. There’s an abundance, or nearly an infinite amount of things, which is great," because it encapsulates the idea that there isn't necessarily a "correct" answer---like I mentioned, it's about infinite options.
